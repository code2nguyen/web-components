A heatmap is one idea in three layouts. Rows are put into cells by two keys, the rows that share a cell are combined by `aggregate`, and each cell is coloured by its value. A calendar is one way to get the two keys, from a date; any two fields are others: a currency against a currency, a pair against a tenor, an hour against a weekday.

| `layout`             | Keys                                                                        | Typical use                                                                        |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `grid` (the default) | `x-field` columns × `y-field` rows                                          | Cross-currency moves, implied vol by tenor, cohort retention, a correlation matrix |
| `calendar`           | `date-field`: a column per week, a row per weekday, labelled by month       | Daily P&L, contributions, incidents per day                                        |
| `calendar-month`     | `date-field`: one month grid per month, with day numbers when there is room | Daily figures read by date: realized vol, bookings                                 |

## Keys and order

`x-bin` and `y-bin` derive a key from a date field instead of reading it as it is: `hour`, `weekday`, `day`, `week`, `month`, `quarter` or `year`. One timestamp binned twice, `x-bin="hour"` and `y-bin="weekday"`, is the general form of the calendar. A timestamp's day and hour are read in the local time zone, or in UTC with `time-zone="utc"`; a plain `YYYY-MM-DD` date is that day in either.

`x-order` and `y-order` order the axes:

- `data`: the order keys first appear in the rows (the default for a plain key);
- `natural` or `natural-desc`: numeric-aware text order, or time order for a binned key (the default for one);
- `tenor`: by maturity, so `ON`, `TN`, `1W`, `1M`, `3M`, `1Y`, `1Y6M`, `10Y` sort the way a curve reads, whatever order the quotes arrive in;
- `value-asc` or `value-desc`: by each column's or row's total;
- `x` (rows only): the rows mirror the columns, for a matrix;
- a JSON list of keys, `x-order='["USD", "EUR", "JPY"]'`, which also adds listed columns that have no rows.

`group-field` bands the rows into labelled groups, G10 and EM pairs or one desk per band, in `group-order`. `diagonal="hide"` blanks the cells whose two keys are equal, as a cross-currency matrix needs. `marginals` adds bars with each column's total along the top (`top`), each row's along the end (`end`), or both.

## Aggregation

`aggregate` combines the rows that share a cell: `sum` (the default), `mean`, `count`, `min`, `max`, `first` or `last`. `count` needs no `value-field`. The marginal totals aggregate the rows of the whole column or row the same way, so a mean stays a mean of rows. The tooltip says how many rows a cell holds, and `cell-click` hands them over in `detail.rows`.

On the calendar layouts, `range` sets the span, a year (`2026`), months (`2026-08/2026-10`) or days, and every day in it without rows is drawn empty, in `--c2-chart__cell__empty--background-color`. Without `range` the span runs from the first day with rows to the last. `weekdays="mon-fri"` drops weekends, as trading days do; `week-start="sunday"` starts the weeks on Sunday.

## Scales

The colour scale is the one [`c2-map-chart`](../map-chart) uses. `scale="sequential"` (the default) shades from `--c2-chart__scale-start--color` to `--c2-chart__scale-end--color` across the data's extent. `scale="diverging"` runs from `--c2-chart__scale-negative--color` through `--c2-chart__scale-mid--color` to the end colour around `0`, with a range symmetric around it, so a loss and a gain of the same size are equally strong. `domain` pins the ends, `[min, max]` or `[min, mid, max]`, so two heatmaps can be compared; `thresholds` turns either scale into stepped classes, and `scale-colors` gives one colour per class.

The default diverging pair is orange and blue, which stay apart for readers with red-green colour blindness. A desk that expects green and red can set them with `scale-colors`, as the gallery's gain and loss card does; keep the stepped key or the cell labels as a second cue.

Each cell's value is written in it when it fits (`cell-labels="auto"`), on every cell (`always`), or never (`none`), in `--c2-chart__cell-label--color` on a light cell and `--c2-chart__cell-label__inverse--color` on a dark one. `unit` is appended to every value; `format` is the property for anything else.

## The colour key

The built-in legend is the colour key, and by default (`legend-detail="histogram"`) it does more than list colours:

- bars above the scale count the cells in each band, so the reader sees how the values are spread before reading a single cell;
- a stepped scale is labelled at the breaks between its classes, a continuous one at round values;
- `scale-poles` names what each end means (`["loss", "gain"]`), and an arrow at an end shows that some values lie past the scale's domain;
- hovering a cell marks its value on the scale and counts the cells in its band;
- hovering a band previews it, dimming the other cells; clicking keeps it, and on a continuous scale a drag keeps a range. The readout gives the count and, for a sum or a count, the total.

`legend-detail="simple"` keeps the bar, its ticks and its ends. `legend-interaction="preview"` only previews, and `none` makes the key a plain picture. The kept range is `scale-filter`, `[from, to)` with `null` for an open end; the application can set it, to share one filter between two heatmaps, and `scale-filter-change` reports the reader's changes. `legend="none"` hides the key.

## Keyboard and events

The plot is one tab stop: the arrow keys move from cell to cell, a calendar's left and right moving a week, Home and End go to the ends of the row, Enter fires `cell-click` and Escape leaves. The focused cell is announced, and a hidden table lists every cell with a value for screen readers. On the colour key, Tab reaches the bands, or the scale of a continuous key, where the arrow keys pick a band, Shift extends it, Enter keeps it and Escape clears it.

`cell-hover` and `cell-click` carry the cell's keys, labels, value, row count and rows; the `cells` property lists every cell with rows, for a table or an export.
