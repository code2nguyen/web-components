`c2-pan-zoom` lays whatever you slot in it out at its natural size and moves it with one transform: an image preview, an SVG diagram, a floor plan, a board. The first view fits the whole content (`fit="contain"`; `cover`, `width` and `none` are the others), never above 100%, and `min-zoom`, `max-zoom` and `zoom-step` bound the zoom. `bounds="content"` keeps the content in view while `bounds="none"` makes a free canvas. A mouse drag pans and glides when released, Ctrl/⌘ + wheel and a pinch zoom to the pointer, and a double-click zooms in one step; `gestures` sets what a plain wheel and one finger do — `cooperative`, the default, leaves them to the page and shows a hint, `pan` pans as in Figma, `zoom` zooms as in a map and `none` leaves only the buttons, the keys and the methods. The viewport is one tab stop: the arrows pan, `+` and `-` zoom, `0` fits and `1` goes to 100%, and focus moving to content out of view pans it into view.

`controls` adds a toolbar (zoom out, the zoom percent, zoom in and fit, plus any buttons in the `controls` slot) and `minimap` an overview that shows while the content overflows: it copies a single slotted image or SVG, takes the `minimap` slot, or draws a plain rectangle. `zoomTo()`, `fit()`, `reset()`, `panBy()`, `centerOn()`, `zoomToElement()` and `reveal()` drive the view from code, `toContentPoint()` maps a pointer to the content, `view-change` reports every frame of a move and `view-settle` its end — the place to save `view`, which comes back from the attribute with no jump.
