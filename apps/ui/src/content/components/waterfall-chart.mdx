A waterfall chart explains how a total got from one value to another: revenue down to net income, an opening cash
balance to a closing one, last year's headcount to this year's. One row is one step: `label-field` names it and the
single series gives the change, so each bar floats from the total before the step to the total after it. Rises are
drawn in the positive colour, falls in the negative one, and a thin connector carries the running total from one bar
to the next. It is drawn by ECharts.

## Import

```typescript tag=CodeBlock
import '@c2n/components/chart/waterfall-chart'
```

This chart draws with [ECharts](https://echarts.apache.org) 6, a peer dependency it loads on first draw: `npm install echarts`.

## Usage

```html tag=UsageBlock,package=@c2n/chart
<style>
  .waterfall-plot {
    width: 100%;
    --c2-chart--height: 320px;
  }
</style>

<div data-label="Income statement">
  <c2-waterfall-chart
    class="waterfall-plot"
    label-field="item"
    total-field="total"
    total-label="Net income"
    value-labels
    data='[{"item":"Revenue","amount":1020,"total":true},{"item":"Cost of revenue","amount":-330},{"item":"Gross profit","total":true},{"item":"R&D","amount":-280},{"item":"Sales & marketing","amount":-170},{"item":"G&A","amount":-70},{"item":"Other income","amount":20},{"item":"Tax","amount":-40}]'
  >
    <c2-chart-series field="amount" label="Amount ($k)"></c2-chart-series>
  </c2-waterfall-chart>
</div>

<div data-label="Cash bridge">
  <c2-waterfall-chart
    class="waterfall-plot"
    label-field="month"
    total-field="opening"
    total-label="Closing cash"
    legend="none"
    data='[{"month":"Opening cash","net":240,"opening":true},{"month":"Jan","net":38},{"month":"Feb","net":-22},{"month":"Mar","net":51},{"month":"Apr","net":-64},{"month":"May","net":17},{"month":"Jun","net":29}]'
  >
    <c2-chart-series field="net" label="Net cash flow"></c2-chart-series>
  </c2-waterfall-chart>
</div>
```

### Data

Rows are plain objects; `label-field` names each bar and the one `c2-chart-series` reads its change, positive or
negative. A row with no value (or `null`) changes nothing and draws no bar.

A row whose `total-field` is `true` is a total: a bar from zero up to the running total at that point, in the series
colour. A total row that also carries a value sets the running total to it first, which is how an opening balance
starts the bridge (the "Revenue" and "Opening cash" rows above). `total-label` appends one closing total after the
last row, so the data does not have to compute it.

The `steps` getter returns every bar with its kind (`increase`, `decrease` or `total`), its change and where it starts
and ends, for a table beside the chart or an export. `buildWaterfallSteps(rows, totalLabel)` is the same computation
as a plain function.

### Colour

Rises take `--c2-chart__positive--color`, falls `--c2-chart__negative--color`, and totals the series colour
(`--c2-chart__series-1--color`, or the series' own `color`). When a rise is bad news, as with costs, swap the two
variables on the element. `--c2-chart__connector--color` and `--c2-chart__connector--width` style the connectors (a
width of `0` leaves them out), and `--c2-chart__bar--border-radius` rounds the bars.

A connector is drawn only where the next bar starts at the level the last one ended at, so a second opening balance
at another level does not get a line leading into it.

### Interaction

The tooltip shows a step's change, signed, and the running total after it; a total shows the total. `point-hover` and
`point-click` report the bar's label and its change (`y`), and `tooltip-change` carries the whole `step`.

The legend lists the kinds of bar the chart draws. Clicking an entry highlights that kind and dims the others; with
`legend-action="toggle"` it hides that kind instead (`setKindVisible('decrease', false)` does the same from code), and
the running total still counts the hidden steps. `value-labels` prints each bar's change over it.

A visually hidden table lists every step with its change and running total for screen readers, and names the plot.
